# Presentation Outline (8 minutes, ~8 slides)

**Slide 1 – Title (15 sec)**
"China Is the World's Biggest Polluter. So Why Do Other Countries Say the US Should Pay?"
Ben Dushnitzky, AIPI 510.

**Slide 2 – Why this matters / audience (45 sec)**
- Climate summits keep getting stuck on "who should pay"
- Audience: general public who hear this debate in the news
- My question: does the answer change depending on how you measure it?

**Slide 3 – Dataset (45 sec)**
- Our World in Data CO2 dataset (Global Carbon Project), ~50k rows, 1750–2024, 79 columns
- Cleaning: split countries from regions/income groups, used years 1850+, dropped missing CO2
- Focus year 2023 (latest with consumption data)

**Slide 4 – Measure 1 & 2: today vs per person (1 min)**
- Chart 1: China = 32% of world CO2 today
- Chart 2: per person, the US emits 14.3 t, 3x the world average; Nigeria 0.6 t

**Slide 5 – Measure 3: history (1 min 15 sec)**
- Chart 3 + Chart 4: US = 23.7% of all CO2 ever, China 15%
- Chart 5 (the surprise): high-income countries = 18% of people, 63% of historical CO2

**Slide 6 – Engineered features (1 min)**
- Fair share ratio = share of historical CO2 / share of population (US 5.6, India 0.2)
- Trade gap % = (consumption − production) / production
- Times world average per person

**Slide 7 – Measure 4: trade (1 min)**
- Chart 6: UK consumption footprint 58% above production; China/India "export" emissions

**Slide 8 – Ethics & limitations (1 min)**
- National averages hide inequality inside countries
- Consumption data is modeled, only ~120 countries
- Borders/colonial history, CO2 only (no deforestation or methane)
- Picking a measure is a political choice, so show all of them

**Slide 9 – Takeaways (45 sec)**
- No single "right" measure; each answers a different question
- Today's total → where cuts matter most now; history/per person → who benefited most
- Next time someone says "look at country X", ask which measure they're using
